Ultimate AMV · Home tab · 3 August 2026

Home stops being a menu
and starts being a dashboard

Two of your tools were missing from Home entirely, and every tile on it was half empty. Home now shows all nine tools, grouped in the order you actually use them, plus a side rail that tells you whether the machine is ready, what you were last working on, and what you recently downloaded.

01 What was wrong

Two separate problems, both visible the moment you open the app.

Frame Interpolation and Dead Frame Remover had no tile on Home. They sit in the left sidebar, they work fine, and Home simply never listed them. If you only ever navigated from the welcome screen, you would not know they exist.

Roughly half of every tile was blank. Each tile was forced to stretch to the full height of its row, but only held an icon, a title, and two lines of text. The rest was empty space, and the "Open" link got pushed to the very bottom of the card, far from the thing it opens.

The knock-on effect: a screen that takes the entire window can only fit eight items, has no room to grow, and tells you nothing you did not already know. Every time you opened the app you looked at a wall of padding.

03 The four stages

The tiles used to carry a small grey label reading MEDIA, DOWNLOADS or APP. That label told you where a tool lived in the code, not what it was for, so it never helped you pick one. The tools are now grouped by when in an edit you reach for them, and the numbers are the real running order.

Where Settings went

Settings is not a stage of an edit, so keeping it in the tool grid muddied the sequence. It moved to a quiet pair of links at the bottom of the right rail, alongside Logs. Both are still one click from Home, and both are still in the sidebar where they always were.

04 The three new panels

Each one answers a question you would otherwise have to go digging for.

PANEL 01

Ready to run

Three lines saying what will actually do the work on your machine right now: whether jobs run on your graphics card or your processor, whether exports get the fast encoding path, and whether any required tool is missing.

Each line has a coloured dot. If anything needs your attention, a button appears that takes you straight to Settings. When everything is fine, there is no button — nothing asks you to do anything.

Why it matters The app can be set to use your graphics card on a machine where none was found. Everything still works, but quietly runs on the processor and takes far longer. Previously the only way to notice was that jobs felt slow.
PANEL 02

Pick up where you left off

Shows the last tool you opened and how long ago, as a single button that takes you back there. It remembers across restarts, and it updates no matter how you navigated — sidebar, icon rail, or a tile on Home.

Opening Settings or Logs does not overwrite it, so a quick trip to change a setting never erases the thing you were actually working on.

Why it matters You close the app mid-edit and reopen it the next day. Instead of scanning the sidebar to remember where you were, the screen already says it.
PANEL 03

Recent downloads

The last four things you pulled in, with the episode and quality, and how long ago. Clicking one opens File Explorer with that file selected.

If you have never downloaded anything, it says so and offers to open the Downloader rather than showing an empty box.

Why it matters Finding a file you downloaded last week meant opening the Downloader and scrolling its history. Now the newest ones are on the screen you already land on.

05 Every message the readiness panel can show

Nine possible lines, three at a time. Green means fine, amber means it works but something is slower or unclear, red means something will actually fail.

Running on your graphics cardYour card was found and is set to do the work. The card's name is shown underneath.
Set to graphics card, but none was foundThe setting says use the card, but nothing usable was detected. Jobs fall back to the processor and take noticeably longer.
Running on your processorDeliberately set to processor. If a usable card was found anyway, the line says so and suggests switching.
Hardware setting unknownThe app could not read its own settings file. Rare, and worth opening Settings to check.
Fast export encoding availableRe-encoded exports use the card's dedicated encoder and finish far quicker.
Exports encode on the processorExports still finish correctly, they just take longer. This is separate from the line above — a card can handle playback but lack the encoder.
All required tools installedNothing is missing.
N required tools are missingNames the missing ones and says scene detection and exports will fail until they install. This is the failure that otherwise looks like a broken video file.
Tool check did not answerThe check itself failed. Suggests restarting the app if things start failing.

06 What it costs to run

Nothing new was added to the engine. The rail only asks questions the app already asks elsewhere.

  • ✓The three checks are the same ones the Scene Splitter and the tools installer already run when they open — reading the settings file, asking which encoders exist, and checking that the tool files are on disk.
  • ✓They run once when Home appears, all three at the same time, and nothing repeats on a timer.
  • ✓Nothing here starts a job, writes a file, or touches your footage. The panels are read-only.
  • ✓If any check fails or times out, Home still renders fully — that line just says the check did not answer, and the tool tiles work normally.
  • ✓Under a custom wallpaper, Home still uses exactly one blurred backdrop layer, same as before. No extra rendering cost.

07 What I left out, and why

A live "jobs running right now" panel. You picked "let me choose" on the panel content, and this is the one I deliberately did not build.

Each tool currently keeps its own progress entirely to itself — when a scene detection or a download is running, only that page knows about it. Putting a live progress panel on Home means every one of the eight tools has to start reporting its progress somewhere shared. That is a real change to how the app is wired, not a Home-screen change, and it would touch every panel in the app.

It is worth doing. It is just its own piece of work, and folding it in here would have meant editing eight files for a change you did not ask for yet. Say the word and I will scope it separately.
