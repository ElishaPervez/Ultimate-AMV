Make the app shrink, not scroll

Right now, dragging the window smaller pushes content out of view and hands you a scrollbar. You want the buttons, text and panels to get proportionally smaller instead. This is the map: what actually happens, why, everywhere it shows up, and the three ways to fix it.

01The short version

Every size in this app is written as a fixed number of screen dots. A button that is 34 dots tall is 34 dots tall whether your window is huge or tiny. So when the window gets smaller, nothing shrinks — the content simply runs out of room, and each panel that runs out of room grows its own scrollbar.

The good news: the app already refuses to scroll as a whole page — the outer frame is locked to the window. The scrollbars you see are all inside panels. That means one global shrink control, applied once, fixes every page at the same time. This does not need 30 separate fixes.

02What actually happens when you drag the window

The app was drawn at 1440 × 900 — that is the size it opens at. Windows lets you drag it down to 1020 × 680 and no further. So the worst case is a window about 29% narrower and 24% shorter than the design. Nothing in the app reacts to that, so the content just gets clipped and scrolled.

03Three reasons it behaves this way

Cause 1

Sizes are frozen

Padding, text size, button height, icon size, card width — 2,498 of them are written as an absolute number of dots. There is no single dial anywhere in the app that could make them all smaller at once. This is the root cause; the other two are consequences.

Cause 2

Every panel scrolls itself

Because the outer frame is locked to the window, each panel was individually given permission to scroll so its content wouldn't get chopped off. 30 boxes now have that permission. It's a sensible safety net — but it means "too small" always resolves as "scroll", never as "shrink".

Cause 3

The rescue rules can't fire

There are 11 "when the window gets narrow, rearrange things" rules. 10 of them are written for widths the window is not allowed to reach. They are dead code. Details below.

04The dead responsive rules real finding

The app has a minimum window width of 1020. Ten of the eleven "narrow window" rules trigger at 980, 900, 900, 860, 720, 720, 680, 480 and below. The window can never be that narrow, so those rules have never once run in the desktop app.

Only the 1100-wide rule (Vocal Separation column layout) can ever run. The other ten are inert.

What this means for you: the sidebar's "collapse to icons automatically when narrow" behaviour, the downloader's "hide the quality picker when cramped" behaviour, and the anime browser's "stack the panes vertically" behaviour all exist in the code and none of them have ever happened. If any of those felt like they were missing — they were.

Once the app shrinks properly, these rules become obsolete rather than broken, because scaling handles the same job more smoothly. They should be deleted, not repaired.

06Three ways to fix it

All three produce the same visible result — the app gets uniformly smaller instead of scrolling. They differ enormously in effort and in what they might break.

Recommended

A · Tell the app window itself to zoom out

~1 day ~60 lines of new code

The app runs inside an embedded browser engine. That engine has a built-in zoom — the same one you'd get pressing Ctrl+minus in a web browser. We watch the window size, and set the zoom to exactly the ratio that makes the design fit. Window is 71% as wide as the design? Zoom to 71%. Everything shrinks together, perfectly proportionally, and the engine does all the layout arithmetic for us.

+Smallest change by a wide margin. No styling files are touched. +Pop-ups and dropdowns are covered for free — they're part of the same page. +All the drag-and-slide interactions (trim handles, the before/after wiper, the video scrubber, the background image cropper) keep working, because they compare a click position against the element's own box and both scale together. I checked all seven of them. +Reversible in one line if you hate it. −Text at 85% or 90% is very slightly softer than at 100% on a standard-resolution monitor. Snapping to 5% steps and staying above 75% keeps this near-invisible; on a high-DPI screen it's undetectable. −One genuine breakage to fix by hand: the embedded anime-site browser in the Downloader is a separate native window laid on top of the app. It's told where to sit using measurements from the zoomed page, so at 80% zoom it would sit in the wrong place and be the wrong size. Fix is a single multiplication, but it must not be forgotten — AnikaiBrowser.tsx:321, 338, 516. −Moving the window between two monitors with different Windows scaling can reset the zoom. Needs a re-apply when that happens.
Alternative

B · Zoom only the app's inner frame, in the styling

~2–3 days

Same idea, but done in the styling rules rather than by the browser engine — we mark one container as "draw everything inside me at 80%". Reaches the same picture, but we then have to hand-fix everything that measures itself against the window rather than against that container.

+Doesn't depend on the embedded browser engine's zoom behaving; works identically in automated tests. +Lets us deliberately exclude things from shrinking — e.g. keep the window's close/minimise buttons at full size. −Anything sized as "full window height" (the outer frame, all the pop-up backdrops, the setup wizard, the error screen — 34 places) would be sized wrong and would itself create a scrollbar. Each needs a compensating adjustment. −Every pop-up is drawn outside the inner frame, so each of the eight pop-up types needs the shrink applied separately, or they'd stay full size on a shrunken app. −The colour-picker's screen eyedropper positions a preview chip using raw pointer coordinates; those stop matching. Needs a fix. −More moving parts, more chances of a subtle mis-sized pop-up we only find weeks later.
Not recommended now

C · Rewrite all 2,498 sizes to be relative

~1–2 weeks + a full visual pass

Convert every fixed size into a size expressed as a multiple of one base number, then shrink the base number. This is what a from-scratch design system would do.

+The most faithful result — text stays perfectly crisp at every size because it's genuinely re-rendered rather than scaled. +Gives fine-grained control: you could shrink padding aggressively while barely shrinking text, which is what a human designer would do. −2,498 edits across 12,469 lines of styling. Hairline borders, shadow offsets, and image-related sizes must not be converted, and telling those apart is manual. −Near-certain to cause a long tail of small visual regressions across all 11 pages. −Delivers the same user-visible outcome as option A for 10× the effort and risk.

Worth doing one day if the app ever needs a real design-system overhaul. Not worth doing to solve this problem.

08How I'd do it

  1. Build a measuring tool first — don't guess A developer-only overlay (hidden in real builds) that outlines every box currently overflowing and reports the exact window size at which each one starts scrolling. Right now the list in §5 is derived from reading the code; this turns it into measured fact, and gives us a pass/fail check for afterwards. It also means you can drag the window around and see instantly what's still wrong, without me guessing.
  2. Wire up the shrink Watch the window's real size, work out the ratio against the 1440 × 900 design, snap it to 5% steps, apply it. Roughly 60 lines in one new file.
  3. Fix the embedded anime browser Three places where the app tells that native sub-window where to sit. Each gets the zoom factor folded in. Verified by opening the Downloader at three different window sizes and confirming the site sits exactly inside its frame.
  4. Delete the ten dead layout rules They can't fire, they're misleading to read, and scaling now does their job better. Keep the one live rule for now and re-check whether it's still wanted.
  5. Decide the floor, then lower the minimum window size Once shrinking works, the 1020 × 680 minimum exists only because the design demanded it. We can drop it — the real question is how small before things get genuinely unusable. My proposal: allow down to 75%, which corresponds to a window of about 1080 × 675, and hold the minimum roughly there. If you want to go smaller than that, we'd want a second look at a few pages.
  6. Walk every page at three sizes with the overlay on All 11 pages plus all 8 pop-up types at 1440×900, 1200×780 and the new minimum. Every scrollbar that appears gets classified as "legitimately long list" or "bug", and the bugs get fixed. This is the review loop.
Each step lands as its own local commit, so any of them can be reverted independently if the result isn't what you pictured.

09Four decisions I need from you

These change the outcome, so I'd rather ask than assume

1 · Should the app also get bigger on a large monitor?

If you maximise on a 2560-wide screen, we could scale everything up to 1.3× so the app fills the screen properly instead of leaving the design floating at its original size with lots of empty space.
My suggestion: yes, but capped at 1.25× — beyond that it starts looking like a kiosk. Say no and we only ever shrink, never grow.

2 · How small should it be allowed to go before scrollbars come back?

Below a certain point, shrinking stops helping and just makes text unreadable.
My suggestion: floor at 75%. Everything above that is comfortable; below it, we let the panels scroll again as a fallback rather than producing 9-dot text.

3 · Should the window's title bar and close/minimise buttons shrink too?

Shrinking them is visually consistent. Keeping them fixed means the close button is always the same easy target, but the title bar will look slightly chunky relative to a shrunken app.
My suggestion: let them shrink — at 75% the close button is still 26 dots, comfortably clickable, and consistency wins.

4 · Do you also want a manual size slider in Settings?

Independent of the window: a "UI size: 80% / 90% / 100% / 110%" control, so someone on a big monitor who wants more on screen can force it. It's a small addition on top of the same machinery, but it's a new setting to maintain.
My suggestion: not in the first pass. Get automatic shrinking right, then add the manual override later if you find yourself wanting it.
